Linnea Olson

ALK-positive lung cancer

Summary

Linnea Olson wrote for years about living with ALK-positive lung cancer. This page records what she and her family published.

It is not evidence that a treatment works, and it is not a claim that she was cured.

She wrote that she was diagnosed with stage IB non-small cell lung cancer in 2005.

She switched her care to Thomas J. Lynch, Jr, MD, and wrote that she was sure that decision saved her life.

After surgery and chemotherapy, the cancer was later classed as stage IV. By 2008, she was told she had three to five months to live.

In October 2008, she became the fourth person with this cancer to take experimental crizotinib.

She later took ceritinib, then lorlatinib.

The tribute on her blog said she had lived with lung cancer for 17 years. That is a fact about her life, not a claim that a drug cured her.

In their own words

Eleven years after being diagnosed with stage 4 lung cancer, my focus is no longer on a cure but rather on living a high-quality life with my disease.

Per The ASCO Post, 25 August 2016, by Linnea Olson, as told to Jo Cavallo. Page opened 13 November 2026.

When I was finally diagnosed with stage IB NSCLC in 2005, the tumor was confined to my lower left lobe and was 5 cm.

Per The ASCO Post, 25 August 2016. Page opened 13 November 2026.

I’m sure the decision saved my life.

Per The ASCO Post, 25 August 2016, about switching her care to Thomas J. Lynch, Jr, MD. Page opened 13 November 2026.

In October 2008, I became the fourth person in the world with NSCLC to take the then-experimental drug crizotinib (Xalkori).

Per The ASCO Post, 25 August 2016. Page opened 13 November 2026.

Although the drug has given me 2 years of disease stability, there are now signs of cancer progression.

Per The ASCO Post, 25 August 2016, about lorlatinib. Page opened 13 November 2026.

How one step led to the next

Timeline

Dates are only as precise as the source. A name appears only when that person or place is named on the linked public page.

  1. 2005

    She was diagnosed with stage IB non-small cell lung cancer. She switched her care to Thomas J. Lynch, Jr, MD, at Massachusetts General. She had surgery to remove the lower lobe of her left lung, then chemotherapy.

    Who they named: Thomas J. Lynch, Jr, MD; Massachusetts General.

    The ASCO Post, 25 August 2016, opened 13 November 2026.

  2. by 2008

    The cancer was reclassified as stage IV. She wrote that she was told she had three to five months to live. A biopsy showed she was ALK-positive.

    The ASCO Post, 25 August 2016, opened 13 November 2026.

  3. October 2008

    She became the fourth person with this cancer to take experimental crizotinib. She later wrote that it gave her three years of high-quality life before she became resistant to it.

    The ASCO Post, 25 August 2016, opened 13 November 2026.

  4. after crizotinib (exact start date not given in the 2016 essay)

    She joined a phase I study of another ALK drug, ceritinib. She wrote that it bought her enough time to enter a trial of lorlatinib.

    The ASCO Post, 25 August 2016, opened 13 November 2026.

  5. as of 25 August 2016

    She still took lorlatinib. She wrote that it had given her two years of disease stability, and that there were then signs of cancer progression. She named her oncologist at that time.

    Who they named: Alice Shaw, MD, PhD; Massachusetts General Hospital.

    The ASCO Post, 25 August 2016, opened 13 November 2026.

  6. 15 November 2021

    She died at home, at the age of 61, with her adult children and family members with her, according to the tribute on her blog.

    a tribute on her blog, 3 December 2021, opened 13 November 2026.
