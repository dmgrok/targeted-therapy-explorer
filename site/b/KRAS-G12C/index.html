Home / Drug pages / KRAS G12C

KRAS G12C is a change in the KRAS gene, which belongs to a family of genes called RAS.

Changes at this spot in RAS genes are often seen in several cancers.

If a test report mentions KRAS G12C, it is noting this gene change. That note is not a diagnosis, and it is not a decision about treatment.

Sources for this introduction

Mutations in the RAS family of proteins are frequently observed across cancer types. The amino acid positions that account for the overwhelming majority of these mutations are G12, G13 and Q61. The different protein isoforms, despite their raw similarity, also behave very differently when expressed in non-native tissue types, likely due to differences in the C-terminal hyper-variable regions. Mis-regulation of isoform expression has been shown to be a driving event in cancer, as well as missense mutations at the three hotspots previously mentioned. The three levels

  • Mainstream — An FDA-approved drug for this gene finding, in this snapshot, that has not been withdrawn as a whole product.
  • In testing — A recruiting study whose entry rules require this gene finding.
  • On the edge — Early public evidence that names a drug. It is not an FDA approval in this snapshot.

Options are grouped by category, then listed alphabetically. That order does not mean one option is better than another. “In testing” is the name used here for recruiting studies.

Exact assignment rule

Mainstream: a therapy is included when the snapshot's targeting rule includes it — the FDA label indication, the FDA pharmacogenomic-biomarker table, or an FDA companion-diagnostic row ties the drug to this biomarker as a selection criterion — and the drug is not a whole-product withdrawal in data/withdrawn.json. Trials: a recruiting ClinicalTrials.gov record whose condition or eligibility inclusion criteria require this biomarker as a selection criterion. On the edge: a CIViC assertion that is PREDICTIVE, that names at least one therapy, and whose ampLevel is present and is not TIER_I_LEVEL_A. TIER_I_LEVEL_A is the highest evidence level recorded on CIViC assertions in this snapshot; lower recorded levels (for example TIER_II_LEVEL_C or TIER_II_LEVEL_D) are treated as earlier evidence. Assertions with no ampLevel are not placed here. Status (ACCEPTED or SUBMITTED) is shown, not used as a filter. Diagnostic, prognostic or oncogenic assertions, and TIER_I_LEVEL_A predictive assertions about a Mainstream therapy, stay in Sources rather than as a separate option. A whole-product withdrawal is tagged Withdrawn and is not Mainstream.

How this tree is built

This tree is built by a fixed computer rule from the FDA label text and from trial condition and title fields in this snapshot, not by hand. For each option, the text is searched first for a cancer type (lung, bowel, head and neck, and others listed in the rule), then for a gene-change subtype the label or trial names (for example EGFR exon 19 deletion or L858R, exon 20 insertion, T790M), then for when the text says the drug or study is used (first treatment, after another drug, after surgery). If the text does not name one of these, that branch is labelled “label does not say” or “trial record does not say”. An option can appear in more than one branch if its source names more than one use. Options in a branch are listed alphabetically. That order is not a ranking.

The rule lives in scripts/tree.py. Cancer types, gene-change subtypes and “when used” phrases are fixed lists in that file.
