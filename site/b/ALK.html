ALK

Anaplastic lymphoma kinase

ALK, short for anaplastic lymphoma kinase, is a gene.

ALK amplifications, fusions and mutations have been shown to be driving events in non-small cell lung cancer.

If a test report mentions ALK, it is recording a finding about this gene. This page lists public FDA companion diagnostics that look for ALK, and the therapies and trials in this snapshot that use ALK as a selection criterion. That mention is not a diagnosis or a treatment decision on its own. How this page is built · This snapshot’s biomarker record

Sources for this introduction

ALK amplifications, fusions and mutations have been shown to be driving events in non-small cell lung cancer. While crizontinib has demonstrated efficacy in treating the amplification, mutations in ALK have been shown to confer resistance to current tyrosine kinase inhibitors. Second-generation TKI's have seen varied success in treating these resistant cases, and the HSP90 inhibitor 17-AAG has been shown to be cytostatic in ALK-altered cell lines. Trials

A recruiting study that requires this biomarker.

A study is in Trials when the existing requiring_trials() rule includes it: it is a recruiting ClinicalTrials.gov record whose condition or eligibility inclusion criteria require this biomarker as a selection criterion.

On the edge

Early evidence, not an FDA approval in this snapshot.

A CIViC assertion is On the edge when all of these hold: it is PREDICTIVE (it names a therapy effect), it names at least one therapy, and its ampLevel is present and is not TIER_I_LEVEL_A. TIER_I_LEVEL_A is the highest evidence level recorded on CIViC assertions in this snapshot; lower recorded levels (for example TIER_II_LEVEL_C or TIER_II_LEVEL_D) are treated as earlier evidence. Assertions with no ampLevel are not placed here. Status (ACCEPTED or SUBMITTED) is shown, not used as a filter. Diagnostic, prognostic or oncogenic assertions, and TIER_I_LEVEL_A predictive assertions about a Mainstream therapy, stay in Sources rather than as a separate option.

Short glossary

Words that appear on these pages and are hard to avoid. These are general explanations, not medical advice.

Biomarker
A measurable feature of a tumour, such as a gene change, that can be used as a selection criterion for a therapy or a trial.
Mutation
A change in the DNA sequence of a gene.
TKI (tyrosine kinase inhibitor)
A type of targeted drug. The letters stand for tyrosine kinase inhibitor. The FDA labels in this snapshot sometimes describe a drug as a kinase inhibitor.
NSCLC
Non-small cell lung cancer, a type of lung cancer named in many of the FDA labels in this snapshot.
FDA
The U.S. Food and Drug Administration, which approves drugs and some tests in the United States. This site uses FDA labels, the pharmacogenomic-biomarker table, and the companion-diagnostic list.
EMA
The European Medicines Agency, which authorises medicines in the European Union. This site adds an EMA match when one is found.
Companion diagnostic
A test on the FDA’s list of authorised companion diagnostic devices, used to identify people who may be considered for a corresponding therapy.
CIViC
A public, volunteer-curated cancer knowledgebase released under a public-domain dedication. Its statements are not regulatory approvals.
Fusion
When parts of two genes join and form an abnormal gene. Reports may mention an ALK fusion or an NTRK fusion.
Exon
A numbered segment of a gene. A report may mention a change in a specific exon.
Recruiting
A clinical trial whose ClinicalTrials.gov record in this snapshot lists the status as recruiting, meaning it was listed as enrolling participants at the time of that record.
ampLevel
The evidence level CIViC records on an assertion, such as TIER_I_LEVEL_A or TIER_II_LEVEL_D. This site uses that field as written. It does not add a meaning beyond what the field itself says.
Indication
The use listed on a drug’s official label: what the drug was approved for, in which disease, and with which test or conditions, as written in the FDA label.
